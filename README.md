# Sustainable Edge Quality Intelligence

Research code, data and manuscript for **"When Does Edge AI Defect Inspection Pay Back Its Carbon? Measured Embedded Inference, Detector Recall, and Alert Design"**.

The study compares, for a functional unit of 1,000 parts in three manufacturing scenarios:

| Regime | Description |
|---|---|
| `N0_NoInspection` | no inspection; every defect escapes |
| `L0_Manual` | 100% manual end-of-line visual inspection |
| `B0`–`B3` | continuous edge-AI inspection (detect-and-divert) under the four alert policies of the companion alert-policy study (Paper A): single-frame threshold, smoothing, smoothing + k-of-N, full policy with incident latch |
| `E1`–`E3` | Paper A's textbook alarm baselines as alternatives to B3: delay-timer, EMA with hysteresis, decision-level fusion |

It accounts for unrecovered material, recycling burden, rework / review / edge electricity, amortised edge-hardware carbon, field escapes and operator hours. Edge power of a real PatchCore pipeline is **measured** on an embedded Jetson Orin Nano (whole module, primary platform) and on a workstation GPU, and the recall/false-positive rates of four detector configurations (PatchCore ResNet-18 at 224 and 448 px, PatchCore WideResNet-50, PaDiM) are **measured** on all 15 MVTec AD and all 12 VisA categories. The alert policies' false-alert rates, alerts per defect, glare alerts, delays and recall losses are **imported with their 95% category-cluster intervals** from a pinned Paper A revision (32a5540), for MVTec AD and the held-out VisA categories, each with independent and temporally correlated (rho = 0.9) frame scores; the importer recomputes them with Paper A's own code and checks them against every value Paper A prints. Everything else comes from a single parameter ledger with explicit provenance classes.

## Key results (central values; all numbers are regenerated by the pipeline)

* **Compute matters only where the benefit is small.** A real PatchCore pipeline at 30 FPS draws 3.98 W for the whole Jetson Orin Nano module in TensorRT FP16 (28 mJ per frame above idle, identical alert decisions to FP32). A workstation GPU alone draws 10.3 W, 10x the energy per frame. In the three reference scenarios the edge cell is at most 1.4% of the carbon benefit and the platform choice changes the benefit by at most 1.3%; across 20,000 sampled products the edge share exceeds 1% for about a third of those that beat manual inspection, mostly products with a small benefit.
* **Against no inspection, edge AI wins** in every Monte Carlo draw of the three scenarios (85.9% of sampled products), and the decision follows a throughput-free rule: inspect when the embodied carbon of the defects produced per hour exceeds the cell's hourly emissions (~28 gCO2e/h).
* **Against manual inspection, the answer is line-specific.** PatchCore's recall at a strict q99 threshold has a median of 0.94 on MVTec AD but 0.57 on VisA (0.79 overall, close to the literature 0.75 for manual inspection). With Paper A's MVTec AD alert measurements, the full policy beats manual inspection in 54-64% of draws (52-59% with correlated frame scores); detector recall explains 85-97% of the variance (Sobol total index). With Paper A's held-out VisA alert measurements, persistence filtering misses 23% of the defects raw thresholding flags and the full policy wins in only 17-33% of draws; smoothing with hysteresis keeps a saving. Pairwise input correlations move these probabilities by at most 1.7 points; partial recycled-content substitution makes edge AI look better (72-85% at s = 0.5).
* **Compute spent on recall pays back.** Doubling the input resolution (448 px) lifts median recall from 0.79 to 0.89 (VisA 0.57 to 0.72, worst category 0.14 to 0.42) for 6.2x the Jetson energy per frame, repaid at least 283-fold by avoided escapes; a larger backbone (WideResNet-50) helps on MVTec AD but not on VisA.
* **Carbon-aware thresholds** (an in-sample optimum, hence an upper bound) lift median recall to ~0.99 and save up to ~8,500 kgCO2e per 1,000 high-value parts without extra reviewers. A 5-per-1,000 alarm budget is attainable in only 5 of 27 categories.
* **Alert design matters.** Single-frame alerting would need 83-229 reviewers. With correlated scores the full policy needs 4-5 reviewers and its alerts become mostly false; on weak VisA defect scores persistence costs recall, so reviewers who learn to distrust them can erase the benefit; released parts should be re-inspected.
* Material, recycling, freight and hardware factors, and defect prevalence where possible, come from public sources (ICE, IAI, IVL, JRC, GLEC, Eurostat, published scrap rates and recycling LCAs).

See `paper/main.tex` and `results/paper_b_generated_metrics.tex` for the exact figures. Journal highlights are in `paper/highlights.txt`; the graphical abstract (`results/figures/graphical_abstract.pdf`) is generated by step 08 from the same outputs.

## Provenance and limitations

Every parameter carries a provenance class in the ledger and in Tables I-II of the paper: measured, imported from Paper A, literature, official data, scenario definition (functional unit, frame rate, line speed, part mass and the recycled-content substitution ratio, which define the reference products and model) or scenario assumption (reworkability, rework energy, manual timing, host power and a few others). The Jetson's embodied carbon is an ACT-model estimate whose upper bound is doubled for the truncation error of bottom-up estimates (Pirson and Bol 2021); the paper reports the multiple at which it would change a conclusion. The paper's appendix holds a reproducibility checklist (meters, boundaries, versions, datasets, seeds). The Jetson measurement covers the whole module but not the carrier board, camera and lighting; the workstation measurement covers the GPU only. Both gaps enter through the `host_power` assumption. The workstation GPU also drives the host desktop under WSL2. On that setup the NVML cumulative energy counter was inconsistent with the power sensor, so power samples are integrated instead. The reported statistics are medians with min–max bounds. See the paper's threats-to-validity section.

## Measuring on a Jetson

```bash
python -c "from pathlib import Path; from src.experiments.edge_export import export_workload; export_workload(Path('runs/edge_export'))"
rsync -a runs/edge_export src USER@JETSON:paperb/          # ONNX graphs, frames, harness
# on the device (JetPack with TensorRT; no PyTorch needed):
cd ~/paperb/edge_export && trtexec --onnx=patchcore.onnx --saveEngine=patchcore_fp16.engine --fp16
cd ~/paperb && python3 -m src.experiments.jetson_benchmark --export-dir edge_export --out jetson_runs --detector patchcore --precision fp16
# back on the workstation:
python scripts/03c_import_jetson.py --host USER@JETSON --update-registry
```

The exported graph contains the whole scoring path (backbone, nearest-neighbour search, upsampling, Gaussian filter, max) and is checked against the reference scorer before export; the harness reports the engine's agreement with the FP32 reference.
