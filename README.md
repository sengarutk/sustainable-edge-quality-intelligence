# Sustainable Edge Quality Intelligence

Research code, data and manuscript for **"Measured Edge-Inference Energy Versus Quality-Driven Carbon, Material, and Workload Effects of AI Defect Inspection in Manufacturing"**.

The study compares six inspection regimes for a functional unit of 1,000 parts in three manufacturing scenarios:

| Regime | Description |
|---|---|
| `N0_NoInspection` | no inspection; every defect escapes |
| `L0_Manual` | 100% manual end-of-line visual inspection |
| `B0`–`B3` | continuous edge-AI inspection (detect-and-divert) under the four alert policies measured in the companion alert-policy study (Paper A): single-frame threshold, smoothing, smoothing + k-of-N, full policy with incident latch |

It accounts for unrecovered material, recycling burden, rework / review / edge electricity, amortised edge-hardware carbon, field escapes and operator hours. Edge GPU power of a real PatchCore pipeline and the detector's recall/false-positive rates on seven MVTec AD categories are **measured**; the alert policies' false-alert rates, alerts per defect, glare alerts and delays are **imported with their confidence intervals** from a pinned Paper A revision; everything else comes from a single parameter ledger with explicit provenance classes.

## Key results (central values; all numbers are regenerated by the pipeline)

* A real PatchCore pipeline (ImageNet ResNet-18, 13,798-patch coreset, real MVTec frames) draws ~10.3 W on an RTX 4050 Laptop GPU at 30 FPS (~0.28 J per frame above idle). Thresholding, the temporal policy and event logging add no measurable power.
* With material, freight and hardware factors from public sources (ICE, International Aluminium Institute, IVL, GLEC, Dell PCF), the edge cell is at most ~2% of the full policy's benefit over manual inspection in the three reference scenarios; the benefit is positive in ≥ 99.8% of Monte Carlo draws and is driven by avoided escapes and material.
* For a lightweight polymer part the margin is thin: the benefit over manual inspection vanishes on grids above ~0.9 kgCO2e/kWh and below ~0.04% defect prevalence (vs no inspection).
* The decision reduces to one exact rule, *inspect when π·β > k* (defect rate × carbon saved per defect > edge-cell carbon per part). Across 20,000 sampled products the full policy beats no inspection in ~85% and manual inspection in ~79% of cases; compute matters where the per-defect benefit is small.
* Carbon-optimal detection thresholds are far more permissive than conventional alarm budgets and, on slower lines, save large amounts of carbon without extra reviewers.
* Alert policy matters more than compute: single-frame thresholding would need tens to hundreds of reviewers; the full policy (incident latch) saves operator time, electricity and carbon relative to it, unless persistence loses more recall than a break-even margin of about two percentage points for high-value parts.

See `paper/main.tex` and `results/paper_b_generated_metrics.tex` for the exact figures.

## Repository layout

```
configs/                 regime structure (policies.yaml), scenario class mix, SQI weight profiles
data/raw/source_registry.csv   single source of truth for every numerical parameter (validated schema)
data/raw/energy_measurements/  raw GPU power traces and per-window records of each benchmark run
data/raw/paper_a_exports/      provenance-tracked snapshot of the Paper A measurements (pinned commit, blob hashes)
src/                     model (quality, sustainability, pipeline), experiments, registry validation
scripts/01-10            pipeline steps (see below)
results/                 generated outputs: processed data, figures, tables, LaTeX macros
paper/                   manuscript; figures/, tables/ and the macro file are copies of results/
tests/                   unit, invariant, provenance and reproducibility tests
```

## Reproduce

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements-lock.txt && python -m pip install -e . --no-deps
bash scripts/reproduce_all.sh            # analysis from the committed measurements (no GPU needed)
bash scripts/reproduce_all.sh --measure  # also re-measure GPU power (NVIDIA GPU + NVML; pip install -e .[bench])
```

| 01 | `01_import_upstream.py` (optional) | refresh the Paper A snapshot from a local checkout (`--ref COMMIT`, `--out PATH`, `--update-registry`) |

## Provenance and limitations

Most parameters are scenario assumptions and are labelled as such in the ledger and in Table I of the paper. The measurement covers the GPU only; host, camera and lighting power enter through the `host_power` assumption. The benchmark used a GPU that also drives the host desktop under WSL2. On that setup the NVML cumulative energy counter was inconsistent with the power sensor, so power samples are integrated instead. The reported statistics are medians with min–max bounds. See the paper's threats-to-validity section.
