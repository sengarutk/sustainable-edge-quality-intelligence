# Sustainable Edge Quality Intelligence

Research code, data and manuscript for **"When Does Edge AI Defect Inspection Pay Back Its Carbon? Measured Embedded Inference, Detector Recall, and Alert Design"**.

The study compares six inspection regimes for a functional unit of 1,000 parts in three manufacturing scenarios:

| Regime | Description |
|---|---|
| `N0_NoInspection` | no inspection; every defect escapes |
| `L0_Manual` | 100% manual end-of-line visual inspection |
| `B0`–`B3` | continuous edge-AI inspection (detect-and-divert) under the four alert policies measured in the companion alert-policy study (Paper A): single-frame threshold, smoothing, smoothing + k-of-N, full policy with incident latch |

It accounts for unrecovered material, recycling burden, rework / review / edge electricity, amortised edge-hardware carbon, field escapes and operator hours. Edge power of a real PatchCore pipeline is **measured** on an embedded Jetson Orin Nano (whole module, primary platform) and on a workstation GPU, and the recall/false-positive rates of two detectors (PatchCore, PaDiM) are **measured** on all 15 MVTec AD and all 12 VisA categories; the alert policies' false-alert rates, alerts per defect, glare alerts and delays are **imported with their confidence intervals** from a pinned Paper A revision; everything else comes from a single parameter ledger with explicit provenance classes.

## Key results (central values; all numbers are regenerated by the pipeline)

* **Compute never decides.** A real PatchCore pipeline at 30 FPS draws 3.96 W for the whole Jetson Orin Nano module in TensorRT FP16 (28 mJ per frame above idle, identical alert decisions to FP32). A workstation GPU alone draws 10.3 W, 10x the energy per frame. The edge cell is at most ~1.4% of the carbon benefit, and the platform choice changes the benefit by at most ~1.3%. On embedded hardware the camera and lighting draw more than inference.
* **Against no inspection, edge AI wins** in every Monte Carlo draw of the three scenarios, and the decision reduces to an exact, throughput-free rule: inspect when the embodied carbon of the defects produced per hour exceeds the cell's hourly emissions (~28 gCO2e/h).
* **Against human inspectors, recall decides.** Measured over all 15 MVTec AD and 12 VisA categories, PatchCore's recall at a strict q99 threshold has a median of 0.94 on MVTec AD but 0.57 on VisA (0.79 overall, close to the 0.75 of manual inspection). Edge AI then beats manual inspection in only 53-64% of draws, and detector recall explains 85-97% of the variance (Sobol total index).
* **Carbon-aware thresholds restore the advantage**, lifting median recall to ~0.99 and saving up to ~8,500 kgCO2e per 1,000 high-value parts without extra reviewers.
* **Alert design matters.** Single-frame alerting would need tens to hundreds of reviewers. On slow lines the incident latch leaves glare dominating the alerts (precision ~10%), so reviewers who learn to distrust them can erase the benefit; releases should be re-inspected.
* Material, recycling, freight and hardware factors, and defect prevalence where possible, come from public sources (ICE, IAI, IVL, JRC, GLEC, Eurostat, published scrap rates and recycling LCAs).

See `paper/main.tex` and `results/paper_b_generated_metrics.tex` for the exact figures.

## Provenance and limitations

Every parameter carries a provenance class in the ledger and in Table I of the paper; the remaining scenario assumptions (reworkability, rework energy, throughput, manual timing, host power) are labelled as such. The Jetson measurement covers the whole module but not the carrier board, camera and lighting; the workstation measurement covers the GPU only. Both gaps enter through the `host_power` assumption. The workstation GPU also drives the host desktop under WSL2. On that setup the NVML cumulative energy counter was inconsistent with the power sensor, so power samples are integrated instead. The reported statistics are medians with min–max bounds. See the paper's threats-to-validity section.

## Measuring on a Jetson

```bash
python -c "from pathlib import Path; from src.experiments.edge_export import export_workload; export_workload(Path('runs/edge_export'))"
rsync -a runs/edge_export src USER@JETSON:paperb/          # ONNX graphs, frames, harness
# on the device (JetPack with TensorRT; no PyTorch needed):
cd ~/paperb/edge_export && trtexec --onnx=patchcore.onnx --saveEngine=patchcore_fp16.engine --fp16
cd ~/paperb && python3 -m src.experiments.jetson_benchmark --export-dir edge_export --out jetson_runs --detector patchcore --precision fp16
# back on the workstation:
python scripts/03c_import_jetson.py --host USER@JETSON --update-registry
```

The exported graph contains the whole scoring path (backbone, nearest-neighbour search, upsampling, Gaussian filter, max) and is checked against the reference scorer before export; the harness reports the engine's agreement with the FP32 reference.
